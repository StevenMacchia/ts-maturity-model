# Steps to the next level

Two concrete steps for moving each area up one level. The assessment builds a roadmap from these, one level at a time, starting with the biggest gaps. When gaps are the same size it starts with crisis response, then regulatory readiness, then detection and prevention.

## Policy and standards

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] Write community guidelines that cover your most serious harms
- [ ] Name one owner for each policy area

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Write internal enforcement guidelines with clear examples for every policy
- [ ] Keep a change log so reviewers know what changed and when

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Stress-test new and changed policies against edge cases before they ship
- [ ] Track reviewer agreement for each policy to find rules that are unclear

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Consult outside experts and affected communities on major changes
- [ ] Publish the reasoning behind significant policy changes

## Detection and prevention

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] Add reporting to every place where users meet or post
- [ ] Start block lists for the words and links behind your top harms

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Match uploads against known CSAM hashes, for example with PhotoDNA or ROOST's free, open-source Coop, and report matches as the law requires
- [ ] Rank the review queue by severity instead of arrival time

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Measure precision and recall for each classifier on a labeled sample
- [ ] Add account and network signals to catch coordinated abuse

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Run an abuse pre-mortem on every new feature before launch
- [ ] Join an industry signal-sharing program, such as the GIFCT hash database or the Tech Coalition's Lantern

## Review operations

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] Make a named team or vendor responsible for the review queue
- [ ] Set a target time to act on user reports

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Set service levels by severity, with round-the-clock cover for child safety and threats to life
- [ ] Write an escalation path to legal, communications and leadership

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Forecast review capacity from volume trends every quarter
- [ ] Score vendors on quality, wellness and surge capacity

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Automate clear-cut decisions and route complex cases to specialists, for example with ROOST's free Coop or Osprey
- [ ] Test a surge plan that adds reviewers within hours

## Quality and appeals

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] Let users appeal every action on their account or content
- [ ] Spot-check a small sample of decisions every week

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Run a weekly quality sample and report accuracy for each policy
- [ ] Give appeals their own queue, a different reviewer and a response target

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Track appeal overturn rates by policy and use them to update guidelines
- [ ] Hold calibration sessions where reviewers decide the same cases

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Calibrate every team and vendor against one shared answer key
- [ ] Give users specific reasons with every decision, including an EU statement of reasons where required

## Crisis response

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] Agree what counts as a crisis and who declares one
- [ ] List who to call in legal, communications, engineering and law enforcement

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Write a playbook with severity levels, roles and an on-call rotation
- [ ] Prepare holding statements and regulator notification templates

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Run a tabletop exercise at least twice a year
- [ ] Hold a blameless review after every major incident and track the actions

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Include executives in at least one exercise a year
- [ ] Add playbooks for elections, AI-generated content and coordinated attacks

## Metrics and reporting

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] Log every report, decision and action with a timestamp
- [ ] Build a simple dashboard for volume and response time

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Choose a scorecard that covers prevalence, speed, accuracy and appeals
- [ ] Review the scorecard with leadership every quarter

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Measure prevalence with a properly sized random sample
- [ ] Publish a transparency report

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Make safety metrics a required input to launch and business reviews
- [ ] Survey users on how safe they feel and track it next to enforcement data

## Regulatory readiness

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] List where you operate and the online safety laws that apply there
- [ ] Name who owns regulatory questions

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Map each legal obligation to an owner, a process and the evidence you keep
- [ ] Set up required reporting, such as reporting CSAM to NCMEC in the US

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Document risk assessments and repeat them when products change
- [ ] Track regulator and law enforcement requests from receipt to response

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Add a regulatory check to your product launch process
- [ ] Respond to consultations on upcoming rules

## Team wellbeing

**Level 1 → 2 (Developing)**

- [ ] Give reviewers access to confidential counseling
- [ ] Tell every new reviewer what they'll see and how to get support

**Level 2 → 3 (Defined)**

- [ ] Set daily exposure limits for the most harmful content
- [ ] Turn on blurring and grayscale by default in review tools (ROOST's open-source Coop has both)

**Level 3 → 4 (Managed)**

- [ ] Survey reviewer wellbeing and track attrition every quarter
- [ ] Write wellness standards into every vendor contract

**Level 4 → 5 (Leading)**

- [ ] Offer support after people leave review roles
- [ ] Make a senior leader accountable for reviewer wellbeing
